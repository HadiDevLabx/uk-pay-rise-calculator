# Pay Rise Calculator UK — How Much of a Pay Rise Do You Keep?

> A £5,000 rise from £45,000 to £50,000 adds £3,600.00 to your take-home. The same rise from £100,000 adds £1,900.00.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

What you keep depends entirely on which band the rise lands in.

| Rise | Gross gain | Net gain | Kept |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| £30,000 → £35,000 | £5,000 | £3,600.00 | 72% |
| £45,000 → £50,000 | £5,000 | £3,600.00 | 72% |
| £50,000 → £55,000 | £5,000 | £2,937.80 | 58.8% |
| £100,000 → £105,000 | £5,000 | £1,900.00 | 38% |

A pay rise never leaves you worse off through the tax bands alone. It can through the loss of a benefit, which is a different mechanism.

## What this does not cover

Crossing £60,000 with children, or £100,000 with childcare, can cost more than the rise is worth. Those are cliff edges outside the tax bands.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
